Express service · skips the panels

A register by November 20

Nobody is going to pick PJ. The programs run panels, the panels run cycles, and the next good cycle is 2027. So we stop applying and start calling. Four tracks run at the same time. Whichever one hands over keys first wins. The Seaport is where the receipts come from, so the Seaport is where we look first.

1

Call the sign

Keys in 2 to 4 weeks · no fee

Walk the main streets in the finder, starting at the Seaport. Every dark door with a sign has a phone number. That number is the owner's own agent. Not a panel. Not a program.

  • Offer: a six week holiday license, percent of gross, 30-day out both ways.
  • PJ brings insurance, the receipts, and the art. The owner brings a key.
  • Ask every broker the same question: what else of yours is dark?
2

Book it

Keys in days · costs rent

Short-term storefront marketplaces list Manhattan and Brooklyn spaces by the day or week. You pay, you get the room. No selection panel. Terms are agreed with the space owner directly.

  • Storefront · xNomad · Popshop
  • This is the floor. It guarantees November 20 if the other two stall.
3

Shop in a shop

Keys this month · percent of gross

A wall, a rack and a register inside a store that is already open on the avenue. The lights are already on. The foot traffic is already in the door.

  • Pitch store owners PJ already knows first. They have seen the work.
  • The store's own lease may not allow it. Ask them to check before anything is signed.

For the owner · print this part

The pitch, in plain terms

  1. 1
    Dark costs you every month
    Every month a storefront sits dark, it costs the owner. An artist in it costs less than the empty room does.
  2. 2
    We have seen this movie
    In the 80s and 90s artists took the dead spaces, made the block worth something, and got priced out by the value they made. They had no paper. This is the paper.
  3. 3
    Your ask stays on paper
    The space reads occupied. The asking rent is never touched, because there is no rent. You keep the right to hand the space to a full tenant on short notice.
  4. $
    The receipts walk in first
    Nobody else asking for your space shows up with nine weeks of real sales. PJ does.
Not on the table: the idea that owners get a tax break for keeping space empty. That is a myth. We do not bring it up.

Read this before you call anyone. The registry says what the owner reported, not what is true today. Walk it. Owner names are not in this data; ACRIS has them. PJ's checklist · in order

Station stops

  1. 1
    Confirm the entity that signs
    The name on the license, the insurance, the tax certificate and the bank account should all match.
  2. 2
    File the sales tax certificate
    20 days before the first sale in a room. Oct 31 at the latest for Nov 20.
  3. 3
    Turn on item tracking in Square
    Every sale is still "Custom Amount." An owner will ask what sold. Right now nobody can say.
  4. 4
    Walk the list
    Use the finder. Seaport and Lower Manhattan first, main streets only. Photograph every sign and every phone number.
  5. 5
    ACRIS the top five
    Get the real owner, not the LLC name on the door. The filing may be older than the last sale.
  6. 6
    Apply to three markets
    The Seaport holiday market, Artists and Fleas, and FAD Market. A booth still sells in December if no door opens.
  7. 7
    Lawyer's fifteen minutes
    On the license one-pager, before it reaches an owner.
  8. 8
    First pitch: the best Seaport door
    Walk the Seaport list and pitch the strongest door with the receipts in hand. 28 Avenue A is the second call: dark in every city filing since 2019, too big for one maker.
